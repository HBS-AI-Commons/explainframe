# What is Erdős problem 643?

## Erdős problem 643

Today we’re looking at Erdős problem six forty-three, a question about when a pattern becomes unavoidable. We’ll explain the version with groups of three, using just six dots to see what the problem is asking.

**Visual description:** The title first introduces the topic. During the spoken introduction, six numbered dots appear, with a group of three highlighted.

## Begin with six dots

Here is the three-element version of Erdős problem six forty-three. Start with six labeled dots. A triple is simply a group of three dots. We are going to collect triples and look for a particular pattern.

**Visual description:** Six dots labeled 1 through 6 stay fixed. A highlighted group identifies one triple.

## Two triples cover six dots

Choose the triple one, two, three, and the triple four, five, six. They do not overlap. Together, they cover all six dots. This gives us one way to split these six dots into two triples.

**Visual description:** Triples 123 and 456 are highlighted in different colors; their union is the same six dots.

## A different split

Now choose one, two, four, and three, five, six. These triples also do not overlap, and they cover exactly the same six dots. The four triples are distinct. If our collection contains all four, we have found the pattern.

**Visual description:** The same dot identities are regrouped as 124 and 356, with the original partition retained for comparison.

## The collection matters

Possible groups do not count unless we actually selected them. Remove the triple three, five, six from this four-triple collection. The second split is incomplete. The pattern we just showed is no longer present.

**Visual description:** A four-row collection loses 356. One partition remains complete; the second has a missing triple.

## How many force the pattern?

With more dots, we can choose many more triples. The question asks how large a collection must be before this repeated split becomes unavoidable. Finding one example is easy. Proving what every sufficiently large collection must contain is the mathematical challenge.

**Visual description:** A larger set of dots leads to many selected triples, followed by the question of a guaranteed repeated partition.
