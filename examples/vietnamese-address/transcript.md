# Why does “you” change in Vietnamese?

## Saying “you” in Vietnamese

Today we’re looking at how people say I and you in Vietnamese. Imagine meeting an older friend for coffee. The words you use can depend on your relationship. Let’s follow one conversation and see how that works.

**Visual description:** The title first introduces the topic. During the spoken introduction, two people appear, labeled You and Older friend, to establish the conversation.

## English hides the relationship

In English, you can say you to an older friend, a younger colleague, or a stranger. Vietnamese often makes the relationship more explicit. Words used for family relationships can also address people outside the family.

**Visual description:** The word you connects one speaker to three people with different relationships.

## Talking to someone older

Take a simple adult conversation. When addressing an older man, a younger speaker might use the term shown here, which also means older brother. The speaker can refer to themself with the term for a younger sibling.

**Visual description:** A younger speaker addresses an older man as anh and refers to self as em. English meanings are displayed.

## Reverse the conversation

Now the older man speaks. He can use the older-brother term for himself and the younger-sibling term for the other person. The people have not changed. Who is speaking has changed, so the English translations switch between I and you.

**Visual description:** The same two people retain anh and em labels, while I and you swap as the speaker changes.

## More than age alone

For an older woman, the older-sister term is another common choice. But this is not a universal age calculator. Familiarity, social roles, setting, and personal preference also matter. Other forms of address exist.

**Visual description:** A new relationship uses chị for an older woman, followed by context factors rather than a rigid lookup rule.

## Learn the pair, not one word

Instead of memorizing a single translation for you, learn how two people address each other in a particular situation. Notice both sides of the conversation. The words tell you something about the relationship as well as who is speaking.

**Visual description:** The paired terms anh and em remain attached to the same people across two directions of conversation.
